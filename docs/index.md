# Predictive Maintenance: Physics vs. Black Box

Can a machine learning model predict milling machine failures better than a few lines of physics?
This project answers that question on the [AI4I 2020 dataset][dataset], and turns the answer into
an interactive maintenance app.

## The short version

A gradient boosting model trained on the raw sensor readings reaches **F1 0.78**. Explainability
(SHAP) showed that it had learned three sharp failure regions. Each one turned out to be a simple
physical law. Written as three rules, they reach **F1 0.92 with 100% precision**: every alarm is a
real failure.

| Approach | PR-AUC | F1 | Precision | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML on raw sensor data | 0.835 | 0.779 | 80% | 76% |
| ML + physics features (random forest) | 0.891 | 0.877 | 94% | 82% |
| **Physics rules only** (3 lines) | 0.852 | **0.917** | **100%** | 85% |
| **Hybrid: rules + calibrated ML** | **0.913** | **0.917** | **100%** | 85% |

The remaining 15% of failures carry no signal in the data: tool wear failures happen at a random
moment, and some failures have no recorded cause. No model can predict those.

Once the costs of errors are taken into account, the cheapest strategy is **physics rules plus
changing the tool before it gets old**. With a breakdown costing ten inspections, this saves about
**77%** of maintenance cost compared with running machines until they fail. It also beats every
pure ML policy.

## What the app does

For one reading of the machine's sensors, the app:

1. checks the three **physics rules** (power, overstrain, heat dissipation)
2. checks whether the **tool** has reached its cost-optimal replacement age
3. shows a calibrated **failure risk** from the hybrid model
4. recommends an action: **stop**, **change tool**, **inspect** or **continue**
5. explains *why*, with plots of the operating point and a SHAP chart

Read [Reading the app](app/guide.md) for a walkthrough of every element, or jump to the
[worked examples](app/examples.md).

!!! note "About the data"
    AI4I 2020 is a *synthetic* dataset, published by Stephan Matzka (2020) under CC BY 4.0. It
    models a milling machine with 10,000 processes and five failure modes. Several of its failure
    modes are generated by exact rules, which is why physics can explain them perfectly. Real
    machines are noisier; the [results page](results.md#what-carries-over-to-real-machines) discusses
    which lessons carry over.
